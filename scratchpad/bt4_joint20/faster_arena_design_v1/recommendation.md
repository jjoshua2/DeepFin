# Faster future recipe evaluation — independent design audit

Recommendation: use only 100 and 400 simulations, use a capped paired GSPRT for future broad strength screens, and retain a small fixed paired core for questions about search scaling. Reuse existing GSPRT mathematics; add a distinct sequential launcher/readout contract instead of weakening the registered fixed-bank reader. Current H20 C400 remains unchanged and unread.

## Concrete future allocation

1. **Broad recipe screen:** candidate versus the current incumbent only, 100 simulations. Proposed logistic-Elo hypotheses H0=0 and H1=+15, alpha=.05, beta=.10; hard cap 500 opening pairs/1000 games. First decision at 128 complete canonical pairs/256 games, then every 64 pairs, plus the final cap. Stop on the first registered boundary; otherwise report INCONCLUSIVE. The beta=.10 tradeoff accepts more missed improvements to speed exploration; use .05 if preserving smaller gains matters more. These are per-test nominal/asymptotic error targets, not whole-program guarantees. The minimum128 is a pragmatic stable-core choice, not a proved universal small-sample guarantee.
2. **Protected higher-search probe:** for a substantively new family with a search-scaling hypothesis (pure BT4, a different teacher mapping, longer horizon), predeclare a fixed 128-pair/256-game 400-simulation probe on the SAME128 opening pairs as the mandatory first128 low-search pairs, regardless of the low-search sign. This is deliberately coarse, aimed at large differences. A 250-pair/500-game probe is reasonable when +/-25 Elo resolution is needed; it should be a conscious allocation, not the default for every tweak. Do not add an intermediate25/50/200 rung unless a specific turnover hypothesis needs it.
3. **Follow-up:** expand only the cell that can change the next research choice. Use a fresh, separately registered fixed bank or sequential test. Do not combine outcome-selected repeats and quote the ordinary fixed-N CI. A mechanistic comparison such as H20/G20 is useful once to answer the target-construction question; repeating it for every new recipe usually spends games without changing selection.
4. **Confirmation:** freeze the selected recipe/calibration, compare it with the incumbent at400 using two fresh paired training seeds (three preferred), initially250 fresh opening pairs/500 games per seed. Report each seed separately and an equal-seed aggregate. Use a paired hierarchical analysis for a training-recipe claim; two or three seeds still give imprecise between-seed variance. Add a matching100-simulation bank only if the search interaction itself remains a deciding question. For a claim about one specific checkpoint, one fixed500-pair bank is an alternative, explicitly conditional on those weights. More games on one seed cannot replace independent training seeds.

## What the actual banks imply

The completed H20/C100 and H20/G100 pair-score variances are .0861370 and .0840271. Their actual500-pair Elo intervals have roughly18–19 Elo halfwidths, not the generic +/-10–15 claim in eval_protocol. Holding those variances fixed as a planning approximation, fixed128/192/250/500 pairs give near-null halfwidths about35/29/25/18 Elo. Thus256 games are enough to identify huge differences but cannot reliably settle a+15 Elo gain. The difference between two budget scores will generally be less precise; alignment may help, and its observed covariance must be used rather than assumed.

A retrospective illustration using the existing local GSPRT implementation on canonical prefixes of these TWO COMPLETED banks, with the proposed0/+15,.05/.10 boundaries and128-then64 look schedule, crosses H1 at320pairs/640games for H20/C and128pairs/256games for H20/G. This was computed AFTER observing final results: it is neither a prospective validation nor an unbiased forecast of savings. A0/+20 sensitivity illustration crossed at the same look boundaries. The retained calculations label this explicitly. The actual1000-game stages cost40.18 and43.26 minutes; saved games do not translate proportionally to saved wall time because compilation and GPU occupancy have fixed costs. H20 training itself cost2.74GPU hours, so faster arenas help but do not dominate the complete research cycle.

## Interpretation and statistical boundaries

A0/+15 SPRT distinguishes two separated hypotheses; crossing H1 does NOT establish a95% lower confidence bound of+15. Gains in the0..15 indifference region can land either way. A lower crossing favors H0 over the chosen positive alternative; it is not proof that the recipe has no useful gain at another budget or horizon. Likewise, an uncrossed cap is not equivalence or a failed family. The earlier fixed-N practical-gain rule (estimate>=15 and lowerCI>0; futility U<15) is a different decision rule. It cannot be applied at every SPRT look using ordinary95% intervals.

If the real question is whether a gain exceeds+15, use a hypothesis with H0 at+15 and an H1 above it, or a sequentially valid lower bound; that costs more. Keep stopped Elo/ordinaryCI descriptive and report the hypotheses, LLR, stopping prefix, and verdict. Do not claim current generalized-MLE SPRT has exact finite-sample Wald guarantees: Fishtest and its cited paper describe asymptotic guarantees. The local implementation uses logistic Elo; current Fishtest ordinarily uses normalized Elo, so its numerical default bounds must not be copied as though the scales agree.

For scaling, analyze D_i = score_400(pair_i) - score_100(pair_i) on the SAME FIXED paired core, keeping both colorings together and resampling aligned opening blocks. A win at400 is useful strength evidence at400; it does not establish that the advantage grew with search. Two separated budgets identify one interaction contrast, not a whole scaling curve. A raw difference of independently reported Elo estimates with independent errors is not the aligned contrast.

## Actual tool readiness and smallest necessary corrections

- `scripts/arena_standard.py:3510` already drains fixed64-pair chunks with max-concurrent-games128 and checks SPRT after each chunk. This is the safest initial execution mode. No new statistical engine is required.
- Rolling SPRT at `arena_standard.py:2284` feeds `complete_pair_scores(game_scores)`, which skips unfinished pairs. Those are arbitrary fast finishers, not a fixed prefix: whole-pair scoring alone does not eliminate outcome-dependent completion selection. Before rolling SPRT adoption, buffer outcomes and process canonical pair IDs in order, checking EACH newly available declared look boundary in order. Bank speculative finished/inflight suffix separately; it cannot enter the deciding sample. Never substitute whichever pairs finished before a deadline.
- The current monitor starts looking at the first chunk. The proposed128-pair first look must be an explicit setting checked by the execution path, or omit that minimum in a separately reviewed protocol; do not pretend the current CLI already supports it.
- `scripts/bt4_joint_readout.py:151` explicitly rejects sequential banks and accepts only fixed250/500 pairs. `bt4_direct_screen.py` binds those exact counts. A future profile needs to validate exact allowed stopping-prefix membership, declared hypotheses, model/search/book pins, first-crossing trajectory and terminal stop reason. Preserve old fixed-bank acceptance unchanged. A cap/deadline with no crossing is inconclusive, not a qualified fixed-N result.
- Existing SPRT resume permits changing hypotheses with a warning. The new experimental profile should reject that for confirmatory interpretation and preserve the original preregistration; a post-hoc hypothesis cannot turn an old bank into a prospective test.
- Correct local verdict wording saying H1 means the candidate is 'at least elo1', and the blanket exact-Wald claim. The scope here is design review, not a complete audit of the existing GSPRT implementation. Focused prospective simulator/consumer checks should qualify the new profile before its first scientific use.
- Update eval_protocol's blanket1000games/BOTHmodes language to separate exploration, search-budget interaction, fixed-checkpoint confirmation, and architecture-throughput testing. Matched-time is useful for architecture/latency changes; it need not accompany every same-architecture teacher-target change.

Formal GSPRT is worthwhile now because most mathematics and arena wiring already exist, and large recipe effects are being explored. Do not spend days building a replacement framework. While its new profile is qualified, a single fixed128- or192-pair low-search screen is a defensible fast allocation aid, with its wide interval and fresh confirmation clearly separated. It is not permission to peek repeatedly at fixed-N intervals.
