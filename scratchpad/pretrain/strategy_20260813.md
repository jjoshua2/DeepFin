# Supervised pretrain + architecture, and why our anti-scaling findings don't apply to it

Written 2026-08-13 (Tier-13 arm B training; CPU-only analysis, no GPU spent).
Prompted by Josh: *"architecture changes can easily change things by 100 Elo, especially
with a bit of post training after the initial pre-training supervised on the fixed dataset."*

## The measurement that decides it: unique positions per GPU-hour

Realized from arm A's 100 iterations (`train_samples_seen`, banked progress.csv):

| | rate |
|---|---|
| training samples/iteration | 60,672 (118 steps × 512) |
| iteration wall clock | ~300 s |
| **training samples / day** | **17.5 M** |
| realized `train_views_actual` | **4.30** ⇒ **~4.1 M UNIQUE positions/day = ~170 k/hour** |

Now the alternative. BT4 (191M, already on disk, already loads in our stack) is inference-only
for labeling. At a conservative 800–3000 pos/s it produces **2.9–10.8 M labeled positions per
GPU-hour** — i.e. **17–64× more unique supervised positions per GPU-hour than the RL loop
produces**, from a teacher stronger than our net. 50 M labels costs **5–17 GPU-hours**.
(Cheaper still: lc0's public T80/T81 training data already ships positions WITH policy+value
targets — zero labeling cost, billions of positions, only a format conversion.)

The RL loop is not slow because training is slow. It is slow because every unique position
must be produced by MCTS **and** labeled by Stockfish at 150–200 k nodes. That is the
bottleneck a supervised corpus removes entirely.

## ⚑ THIS INVERTS "SCALE ROWS NOT PARAMS" — that finding is CONDITIONAL ON STARVATION

Two of our standing conclusions were measured at ~170 k unique positions/hour:

- [[scale_rows_not_parameters]] — scale data, not parameters;
- [[capacity_confirmed_smaller_net_absorbs_more]] — the smaller net absorbs more.

Both are correct *given the data rate we had*, and both are exactly what you expect when a
63M-parameter model is fed 4 M unique positions/day: parameters are not the binding
constraint, data is. **Remove the data constraint and the sign flips** — under a 50–500 M
position supervised corpus, capacity becomes binding again and the architecture question
becomes live. So Josh's claim and our own "don't scale params" finding are not in conflict;
they apply to different regimes, and nothing we have measured speaks to the regime he is
proposing. That is worth stating in the ledger as a scope limit on both findings, because
they currently read as unconditional.

## What our architecture actually is, vs BT4

Ours: 512-dim × 16-layer × 16-head, **63.08 M** trainable, per-layer smolgen (26.7 M = 42.3 %
of params), non-uniform `ffn_mult` 1.5→1.9. BT4: ~768-dim × 15-layer, ~191 M, smolgen.

**Same family** — both are lc0-lineage attention nets with smolgen. So I would push back
slightly on "architecture" as the lever *by itself*: the topology gap to BT4 is mostly SIZE,
not a missing mechanism. The honest statement is the conjunction Josh already made —
**architecture change is worth ~100 Elo *given* a supervised corpus that can feed it**;
alone, at 4 M unique positions/day, a 3× bigger net would underfit exactly as our own capacity
experiment predicts.

## The sequence, and where the anti-engine thesis lives

1. **Supervised pretrain** a larger net (768×15 as the natural step) on a fixed external
   corpus — lc0 public training data (free, huge) and/or BT4-labeled positions from PGN
   databases + our own bank. Target: general chess strength. Judged by a paired arena vs the
   current 63M net, where a real architecture+data step should clear our ±22 Elo instrument
   by a wide margin — for once the instrument is adequate.
2. **RL post-train** on the existing anti-engine curriculum: SF-exploitation, blind-spot
   seeds, the PID difficulty servo. **This is where the anti-engine specialization happens,
   and it is unchanged** — everything we have built for it (curriculum, seeds, PID, target
   pipeline) applies to a stronger base net.

The strategic claim behind the ordering: you cannot exploit Stockfish's fortress/horizon
blindness from several hundred Elo below it. General strength is the substrate; the
anti-engine objective is the fine-tune. Our current lineage has been trying to learn both at
once from a self-referential signal, which is the fixed point everything this week measured.

## Risks and honest caveats

- **A BT4-distilled net plays like lc0, not like an SF-exploiter.** Post-training is the
  answer, but the size of the post-training budget needed to re-acquire the anti-engine
  behaviour is unknown. Mitigation: keep the current lineage alive as the control; the
  pretrain is a parallel track, not a replacement, until an arena says otherwise.
- **Encoding.** Ours is 175-plane `v2_threats`; lc0 data is 112-plane. Positions can be
  re-encoded from FEN into our format (the encoder is ours and already handles both), but the
  conversion is real work and is where a silent defect would hide — cf. the lc0 policy index,
  where "1858" on both sides agreed on only 46 of 1858 slots.
- **This is a weeks-scale project**, not a one-window experiment, and it competes with the
  Tier-13/14 queue for the single GPU. It should not start mid-Tier-13.
- **Our own negative results on input features and reweighting still stand** — this proposal
  does not revive them; it changes the data regime, which none of them tested.

## Cheapest decisive first step (proposed, not launched)

Before any pretrain: **distill BT4 into our EXISTING 63M architecture on ~5–10 M BT4-labeled
positions (~2–4 GPU-hours) and arena it against the current net.** This is the whole thesis in
miniature and it separates the two claims:
- if the 63M distilled net beats the current 63M net by ≫22 Elo, the bottleneck is DATA/teacher
  and the pretrain plan is validated before we spend weeks on a bigger net;
- if it does not, the bottleneck is architecture or the anti-engine objective itself, and the
  bigger-net step needs a different justification.

Either outcome is decisive at a cost of a few GPU-hours, and it reuses the BT4 adapter and the
arena harness we already have.
