## What A–E actually optimize

D and E each have schema-2 immutable target-overlay storage qualification (D receipt SHA-256 `5f356484cf46af01c707f431ab21d65e910c723d9d813eb5a630e639449f227f`; E receipt SHA-256 `4325d6bb319d278d5c844b0e16a82a3211dab1224d9c6443d0bbc25b82af1f03`); schema 2 here is the overlay schema, distinct from raw SF row schema 3. The E qualification explicitly says storage-only, requiring separate recipe admission. The A–D builder is `scripts/bootstrap_factorial_targets.py`; the E builder is `scripts/bootstrap_sffree_targets.py`. They perform no inference, using prebanked teacher shards and base seals.

The Ceres dual is `0.6 softmax(value/0.55) + 0.4 softmax(value2/1.5)`, a registered mathematical analogue, not a native FP16 getter-parity claim. C/D are approximately equal SF/BT4/Ceres thirds, but first normalize the previously float16-stored V50, then cast the new mixture to float16; they are not bitwise a fresh raw-thirds mixture. B/D/E reuse already T=0.5 stored BT4 at temperature 1 in the second mixing step; it is not sharpened twice. The value order is W,D,L from side to move for the native BT4 named `/output/wdl` probability head and the declared Ceres `value`/`value2` logits contract. The actual Ceres model's semantic order is a producer contract, not independently established from model internals by this static audit.

Frozen `configs/lc0_positive_control.yaml` has `sf_wdl_frac: 0`, `search_wdl_frac: 1`, and documented `game_frac: 0`; the study plans invoke that config. The raw game result is stored in `wdl_target`, while SF root cp converted by `cp_to_wdl_array` is stored in `search_wdl`, then rewritten by the above mixtures. Thus a six-man terminal result is **not directly mixed into the A–E optimized value channel**. It can affect eligibility, shared position distribution, and `wdl_target` (unused at this configured blend). This distinction is central to the old-data impact assessment.

## Source generation and Syzygy

All 35 pinned base summaries describe raw schema-3, history-repetition-fixed source cohorts, banked minimum 7 pieces. They partition as run03 18,910,484 rows, run06 G10 25,827,015, and run07 G10 13,353,189. Source run03 repaired summary and run06/run07 manifests all declare maximum Syzygy adjudication at 6 pieces and the configured pair `syzygy_3-4-5:syzygy_6`. Run06/run07 manifests have `complete:false` because qualified selected subsets were derived; do not call the whole raw runs complete. Run03 repaired summary has `run_finished:true`. The 35 base summaries report 1,354,417 dropped no-result rows in aggregate, rather than fabricated draw targets.

In the frozen `gen_sf_rooted_corpus.py` path, each game checks `board.is_game_over(claim_draw=True)` first, then probes local Syzygy at <=6 before a Stockfish search; rows bank only at >=7. If a <=6 probe is unavailable, play continues and increments an unavailable count. A max-ply game without a result carries `None`, and `derive_corpus_targets.py` drops its rows. `refuse_unopenable_syzygy` checks both configured directories at startup, but individual missing material still returns `None`. StockfishUCI also sets the configured `SyzygyPath` before ready; the checked driver does not explicitly set `Syzygy50MoveRule` or probe-limit options, and this audit has no realized per-search TB-hit measurement. SF search guidance at >=7 can therefore involve its Syzygy configuration, but its exact internal probe coverage is not independently proven here.

Shared `tablebase.py` maps Syzygy ±1 cursed/blessed WDL to theoretical decisive game results. Natural claimable results take precedence at loop top, including claim-aware 50-move/threefold cases. Before a draw becomes claimable, theoretical WDL may disagree with the result achievable under the current halfmove clock; a WDL-only adjudication does not check remaining DTZ against that clock. This is a **conditional semantic concern**, not a confirmed count of wrong old games. It does not make a <=6 training input row: no such row is intended to bank. At a 7-piece root, neural BT4/Ceres predictions remain intentionally raw teacher labels; their disagreement with later exact outcomes is not by itself a labeling bug.

Evidence limit: the generator gate and 35 manifests/summaries establish >=7 as the source contract. The frozen deriver verifies each row's `piece_count` against its FEN but does not independently assert `piece_count >= 7`; this audit did not decode all 58M rows to census that invariant. The raw source manifests are producer declarations, not a complete per-row independent proof. No invalid <=6 accepted row was found.

## Mapping, precision, and joins

Raw SF schema-3 rows carry `input_key` for the actual float32 history tensor. The deriver verifies every emitted row's key; all 35 summaries report their emitted rows as schema 3 with `input_key_verified == rows_written`. Replay `x` is float16 and cannot reconstruct the original float32 key; `row_provenance.npz` in newer derived shards carries `(source_namespace, source_shard, source_row)`, original input key, and post-storage key separately. Original run03's historical BT4 policy collection predates that full raw-row adaptation. Its sidecar binds to derived stored-x fingerprints, policy-array hash, source shard, model SHA, output name, providers, remap provenance, and encoding; it does **not** stamp a per-shard full stored-x digest or original raw input-key replay. This inherited historical lineage is explicit in the collector/builder contracts, not evidence of a found mismatch. G10 adapted policy shards add raw physical-row provenance and full stored-x/source-summary hashes.

BT4 policy `/output/policy` is converted to legal compact LC0-1858 indices using a reconstructed board; collection verifies reconstructed legal moves equal the source legal mask. The Ceres collector converts the stored x to TPG bytes, gathers legal Leela policy slots from TPG context, and stores compact legal logits in source row order. Materialization checks source array hashes, row indices, game/ply identities, full legal roster, and per-row TPG feed digests before mixing. Policy normalization preserves BT4 zero support and sets illegal entries to zero; output becomes float16.

The native BT4 value banks use the named `/output/wdl` **probability** head, native float32 WDL, and a per-row digest of the exact post-cast LC0 feed from stored x. E rechecks those digests, source array hashes, game/ply IDs, attrs and model binding. Ceres stores native float16 policy and dual value logits; materialization softmaxes them in float64 then stores mixtures as float16. The Ceres sidecars carry model and producer hashes, realized provider proof, source storage binding, row order, legal indices, and exact uint8 TPG feed digests. These joins are positional only after strong source/teacher checks. Distinct physical rows may share a quantized stored input key without being silently coalesced.

## Impact and bounded follow-up

No confirmed A–E teacher/source join, WDL POV, legal mapping, mixing-weight, or overlay-schema defect emerged from this static audit and inspected sample attributes. The original-run03 BT4 policy provenance is weaker than later adapted G10 provenance, but it is a disclosed historical boundary, and the E native WDL/Ceres banks independently validate their stored feeds. Storage qualification proves bytes/overlay membership, not model semantic correctness or arena strength.

An independent follow-up to a D/E policy **storage-content** hash mismatch checked cohort00/shard_000000 in full: both decoded float16 `policy_target` arrays are byte-identical over all 8,192 × 1,858 entries (streamed uncompressed SHA-256 `ff1a8215e7c7b684c67ce3fb77e9fad4b6a317c6eaf785a8c4c3baf6a184575a`; zero differing cells). Their `.zarray` metadata and chunk sizes match, while 14 of 16 compressed Blosc chunk payloads differ. On 16 matched physical rows, independent calls to the frozen D and E `mixed_targets` functions reproduced all four stored policy/WDL arrays exactly; D/E policies matched each other while their value targets intentionally differed. The storage hashes therefore do not imply differing training policy targets in this shard. This check covers one shard, not all 7,108 shards; a cross-corpus logical-array parity check can be sampled before making a wider claim.

The Syzygy clock concern could change retrospective game outcomes and which common source games/positions were retained, but it does not directly alter the prepared A–E value mixtures under the frozen game-fraction-zero config. Its frequency and whether any selected game is affected remain unknown. A bounded audit should inspect source terminal adjudication FENs and halfmove clocks by source-qualified game identity, plus producer counts of unavailable probes, on already banked raw rows. If affected games exist, repair can separately relabel/qualify retrospective `wdl_target` for a future nonzero-game-fraction consumer; an exact source-distribution repair may require re-generation because changed termination changes subsequent positions. For A–E, do not rewrite native BT4/Ceres raw heads to tablebase outcomes; any exact TB supervision should be an explicit additional target overlay with its own rule convention and separate experiment. Preserve the current E run while the bounded audit establishes actual impact.
