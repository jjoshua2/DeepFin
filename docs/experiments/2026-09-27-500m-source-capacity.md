# Full-prefix BT4/Ceres source pilots and the 500M capacity boundary

**Readout, 2026-09-27.** The completed source pilots show that generating BT4 and Ceres positions with strict six-piece adjudication is feasible, but the measured stage rates do **not** yet support a 500M-row, 30-calendar-day claim. Neither bank has been deduplicated and admitted with final selected policy/value labels, packed on the external drive, and read through the trainer. The rows below are **selected before deduplication**, and new source, target, corpus, and owner-reuse credit remain zero.

## Completed source and strict-readback measurements

Both pilots used complete games from the same 256-root full-history, exact 16-legal-move-prefix schedule. Natural six-piece endings used the strict rule50-aware Syzygy WDL/DTZ outcome path. The BT4 source produced 4,096 complete games; its first parent ended `INCOMPLETE` because the strict-replay child rechecked a five-minute launch decision after source generation. A separate, independently reviewed CPU recovery replayed all attempts and physical calls without rerunning source generation. Ceres v11 produced 2,048 complete games and completed strict all-attempt and physical-ledger readbacks under its passing zero-credit source operator.

BT4's recovery split was 373.496 seconds strict replay, 262.610 seconds physical-ledger readback, and 19.105 seconds of other setup and verification. Ceres's split was 267.885 seconds strict and 172.773 seconds ledger. These rates divide each bank's selected rows by its measured source plus readback wall. They are single-bank diagnostics with different game counts, not unique admitted throughput or a sustained production-frequency comparison.

The BT4 ledger recorded 15,387 variable-size calls of 1–64 rows, averaging 29.447 real rows. **5,329 calls had at most eight rows**, while 6,933 exceeded 32 and 1,488 were full 64-row calls. A previously circulated “92.02% utilization / at most 7.97% fewer calls” calculation incorrectly treated these variable-size calls as fixed32; it is not a valid packing bound. The short-call tail warrants a source-wall scheduling test with exact game and output parity. Ceres's 8,204 genuinely fixed32 calls used 98.05% of slots, leaving at most 1.95% fewer calls; that is a **call-count ceiling, not a wall saving**. Ceres's source actor spent 654.001 seconds in bound-session host work, 521.728 in root/outcome, and 470.699 in sink/readback within a 1,692.399-second phase account. Splitting and safely overlapping those large non-inference phases is also promising, but no production overlap has passed.

A separate read-only verification proxy on Ceres's saved 32 ZIPs found a 127.830-second mean difference between a repeated full decode/readback and a compact archive rehash. It did not exercise publication rename or the actor path, so it grants no source saving. A fixed 4,096-row selected-label A/B/A/B reduced outer arm wall by 17.76% when recursive aggregate-size scanning moved to a five-second cadence with exact raw/feed/target parity; [that readout](https://github.com/jjoshua2/DeepFin/pull/919) applies only to its small selected-label bank and does not change the source-pilot rates here.

## A transparent 30-day sensitivity, not a forecast

Suppose all **58,090,688 legacy E rows** qualify for inclusion, leaving **441,909,312 new rows**. Assign one third of new generating positions each to shallow SF d6/c8, BT4, and Ceres; route one selected BT4 or Ceres teacher per new row, half to each, for both policy and value; and train one 500M-row visit at the completed Selected-E trainer reference rate of 1,281.357 rows/s. At the older SF reference of 241.7057 eligible rows/s, generating its one-third share would take **7.054 ideal CPU days**. Assuming that CPU work overlaps GPU work completely, the measured BT4/Ceres source-plus-readback rates and inherited selected-label rates yield **39.587 ideal serial GPU-stage days** before unique-row loss, packing, retries, or a cold external-drive training read.

At an assumed 95% GPU duty cycle, 30 calendar days provide **28.5 ideal GPU days**, an illustrative gap of **11.087 days**. Even granting mathematically perfect overlap of BT4 and Ceres CPU replay/ledger behind later sole-GPU source work, while retaining perfect SF CPU/GPU overlap, would leave **34.203 ideal GPU-stage days**, still **5.703** beyond that budget. Perfect overlap is not measured or attainable in full: the last replay tail, CPU and external-disk contention, and failed/retried games consume time. Changing the source shares or selected-teacher mix could move the arithmetic materially, but their quality and admitted yield are not established. The 145,025 eligible positions in a saved SF d6/c8 bank do not yet meet this pilot's complete-prefix/Syzygy source contract. Stockfish may still generate a source share without assigning shallow SF value targets.

The Selected-E trainer rate is an observed one-epoch rate on the older 58M corpus, not a verified 500M external-drive rate. Packed old-E external loading had one favorable cache-affected warm comparison; it does not price the current base-plus-overlay format, first read, or mixed-source pack. No measured optimizer in this document closes the remaining month-scale gap.

### Generating-teacher output reuse is a high-impact conditional lever

Both neural source banks retain the generating model's root outputs: BT4 stores compact root policy and native WDL; Ceres stores legal policy logits and its two value heads. Those saved observations could replace a later teacher call **only** after the admitted row's history/feed, legal map, model/head bytes, calibration and stored policy/WDL target bytes match a fresh selected-teacher reconstruction. The current pilots have not passed that same-call owner proof, and deduplication may change the accepted source shares. Reuse therefore has **zero measured throughput credit**.

The table holds the illustrative new-row thirds, half-BT4/half-Ceres overall selected-teacher marginal, 800.19 BT4 and 329.64 Ceres selected-label rows/s, and all other assumptions above fixed. It changes only which *new* rows need an offline teacher call; the generating model's inference stays charged to source generation. The serial and perfect-overlap columns subtract the calculated label saving from the preceding 39.587- and 34.203-day baselines, so their last decimals inherit those rounded baselines.

For the source-matched illustration, BT4-source rows select BT4, Ceres-source rows select Ceres, and the one-third SF-source rows split equally. This retains a half/half *marginal* teacher mix only if admitted source shares really are equal thirds. It changes the conditional teacher-by-source distribution and may change strength even if the marginal mix is unchanged. A controlled training comparison on the same admitted mixed-source rows must decide that question; a byte-parity test alone cannot. The 26.900-day figure lies under the illustrative 28.5-day ideal GPU budget, but leaves only 1.600 ideal days for every unmeasured loss and assumes unattained perfect CPU readback overlap. It is a feasibility sensitivity, not a month forecast or a reason to alter the already frozen independent route in the first admission pilot.
