# Overlap factorial target preparation with baseline A training

Status: independently reviewed and approved; safe stage handoff requested, queue adoption pending its completion.

The serial queue otherwise waits for all B/C/D targets before starting A, although A uses the existing unchanged 58,090,688-row, 7,108-shard V50 corpus. A combined coordinator runs CPU target preparation and fresh baseline A concurrently. This changes scheduling only: A retains its exact trainer arguments, seed 121, output path, initialization anchor, one-epoch row order, frozen runtime and hourly recovery checkpoints. B/C/D still require the original full target-preparation completion and their overlay qualifications.

A separate `COMPLETE_FACTORIAL58_BASE_A` receipt validates all 35 immutable base seals, ordered roots, summary pins and exact totals. It is not a claim that changed targets are complete. The coordinator starts A only after this independent base qualification; it succeeds only after both original preparation and A have exited successfully and produced their valid receipts. The two arenas that use A bind its successor plan and successful terminal explicitly.

The existing preparation wrapper retains its stage handoff and writer lock, including inheritance by separately sessioned workers. A inherits the GPU and coordinator ownership locks into its training child. Linux parent-death signaling, child-subreaper adoption and descendant cleanup protect the combined lifecycle. These are distinct locks: preparation grandchildren retain the preparation writer lock, not the coordinator lock.

Startup requires 40 GiB available RAM; sustained floor 32 GiB; combined descendant RSS cap 24 GiB; disk reserve 80 GiB. CPU preparation remains on cores 2,3 with low CPU/I/O priority and two numerical threads. The prior exact 58M sampler reported 6.10 GiB peak working set and 5.24 GiB peak decoded arrays; observed preparation worker RSS was 655 MiB. The total outer cap 140,220s preserves both original worst-case budgets including handoff and disk pauses; expected overlap is roughly 11h, subject to I/O contention. No numerical speedup is claimed before measurement.

Independent review checked command equality, donor bindings, lock/cleanup behavior and the actual operator's acceptance of all three successor descriptors. Six CPU tests passed: cleanup for either failed role, inherited lock after supervisor SIGKILL, real base-A admission and actual command identity, immutable publication, and cleanup of a worker orphaned by a killed supervisor. Ruff and diff checks pass.

Adoption requires all four affected items still queued. Under the operator lock it checks their exact old definitions and writes/fsyncs the permanent handoff marker; it releases that lock while waiting for the current whole stage. Only after acquiring preparation ownership and rechecking the queue does it replace preparation and the two arena descriptors, remove the standalone A item, and preserve every other entry. If original preparation starts first, adoption aborts.
