# Persistent Stockfish scalar-value cost screen

**Readout, 2026-09-29.** On 10,000 frozen, history-bearing Stockfish-origin positions, eight persistent single-thread engines with cold transposition tables delivered **363.06 d6, 316.85 d8, and 259.59 d10 scalar values/s** with Hash8. Hash64 delivered 216.38, 203.12, and 177.32 values/s on the same rows. The Hash8 run took 295.339 s versus 475.472 s for Hash64 across both one- and eight-engine arms, a 37.89% observed total-wall reduction. This is a conditional cost screen for a possible SF **value** target; it does not measure a policy correction, playing strength, admitted corpus yield, or production-wide 500M throughput.

| Depth | Hash64, one engine | Hash8, one engine | Hash64, eight engines | Hash8, eight engines | Hash8/64 eight-engine rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| d6 | 99.41/s | 193.51/s | 216.38/s | **363.06/s** | 1.68× |
| d8 | 102.48/s | 162.11/s | 203.12/s | **316.85/s** | 1.56× |
| d10 | 83.37/s | 126.06/s | 177.32/s | **259.59/s** | 1.46× |

Each rate uses the **inclusive wall of a complete 10,000-row fixed-depth pass**, including resets, searches, parsing, output serialization, barriers and the file hash readback. The one-engine arm ran d6→d8→d10 and the eight-engine arm d10→d8→d6, with separate startup and teardown in arm wall. Within the eight-engine passes, the sum of per-engine reset time fell from 242.80/237.00/223.97 s at Hash64 to 122.57/123.63/115.90 s at Hash8 for d6/d8/d10. Search sums also changed. Because Hash64 ran before Hash8 on one host and retained tablebase mappings across rows, these observations do not isolate a causal reset-only speedup or establish a stable capacity rate. Eight engines were only about 1.8–2.2 times the corresponding single-engine Hash64 rate; cold Hash64 clears are a plausible contention source, not a proved sole cause.

The source frame was the closed-game `generation_throughput_20260920/d8_c4` bank: 38,615 qualified rows in 106 progress-listed shards and 200 `(worker, game)` identities. One interrupted unlisted shard was excluded. A fixed hash ranking of `(source, worker, game, ply)` selected 10,000 rows without phase oversampling; the selected rows had zero duplicate input keys, 1,089 opening, 6,525 middlegame and 2,386 endgame positions. The same roster was used for all six passes of both runs. The historical banked comparator is the **rank-one score from a full-width d8 search over every legal move**. It is a within-frame proxy, not scalar-d8 ground truth or deep truth.

The [main-based tool](../../scripts/benchmark_sf_scalar_persistent.py) sends the full legal UCI history from an irreversible history root, rather than a bare current FEN. Each unrelated row/depth receives `ucinewgame`, clearing the transposition table while the qualified binary retains Syzygy cache. The driver requests and capability-checks `Syzygy50MoveRule=true`, `SyzygyProbeLimit=6`, and `SyzygyRetainOnNewGame=true`, with a `readyok` barrier, one thread per engine, and the historical D calibration (`cp_to_wdl`, slope 0.006, draw width 120). It saves raw CP or mate, native UCI WDL, calibrated D-style WDL, best move and nodes separately. UCI does not attest the engine's effective option state. The physical plan used an eight-CPU affinity, an observed 8 GiB RSS refusal limit, a cooperative 7,200 s wall cap, a 15 s per-search tripwire, and a 128 MiB output cap; both runs ended cleanly. The Hash8 plan differed from the reviewed Hash64 plan only at `hash_mb` 64→8.

Raw comparison found **no best-move, mate, or native UCI WDL change** on any of the 10,000 rows at any depth. D-calibrated value and CP were identical at d6 and d8. At d10, **two rows** changed CP and calibrated value in both engine arms: a 13-piece middlegame position moved from −431 to −443 cp (absolute calibrated `win−loss` change 0.01051), and an eight-piece endgame position from −677 to −583 cp (change 0.03057). Neither was a six-man terminal. One d8 and five d10 rows per arm changed node count. The mean absolute d10 calibrated `win−loss` change over all rows was 0.00000411, with p99 zero. Hash8 is therefore a measured faster **numerical profile**, not byte-identical to Hash64 at d10. Native UCI WDL remaining equal in those two rows does not make it interchangeable with the historical D calibration.

Against the banked full-width d8 proxy, the mean absolute calibrated `win−loss` gaps were 0.03065 at d6, 0.02403 at d8 and 0.02019 at d10; best-move agreement was 49.70%, 52.78% and 55.06%. A descriptive game-cluster bootstrap over the 200 source games put the d8-minus-d10 absolute-gap reduction at 0.00385, 95% interval [0.00321, 0.00447]. Search width and depth both differ from the reference, so this proxy cannot rank the true quality of scalar values or infer Elo. No adaptive refinement fraction or threshold was tested.

For capacity sensitivity only, suppose one-third of a hypothetical 500M-row corpus must be newly generated as SF-origin positions at **241.7 rows/s, an illustrative input quoted in earlier capacity discussion, not a rate verified by this screen**, and the measured Hash8 eight-engine scalar rates transfer unchanged. It is not interchangeable with a separately recorded nominal c8 generation rate. Generating 166.7M SF-origin positions takes 7.98 ideal CPU days. The generation and scalar passes use the **same eight-worker CPU pool**, so their times add; a 95% duty factor is applied to the sum:

| Scalar depth | Generate + label all 500M | Generate + label 333.3M new values if SF-origin root scores qualify for reuse |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| d6 | 25.18 calendar days | 19.59 calendar days |
| d8 | 27.63 calendar days | 21.22 calendar days |
| d10 | 31.87 calendar days | 24.05 calendar days |

The 333.3M case requires separate validation of generating-owner history, calibration and recorded engine/value profile; acceptable numerical or legacy-profile differences must be tested. Byte identity would be required only for an identity claim. The source-generation rate, mixed-source distribution, dedup yield, retries, current GPU label work, packing and training are outside this scalar screen. These numbers are sensitivity arithmetic, not a capacity reservation or a 500M forecast.

The next bounded optimization question is whether a scalar-only UCI pipeline can enqueue `ucinewgame`, `isready`, full-history `position`, and `go` together, **retaining the `readyok` barrier while removing the host roundtrip before `position`/`go`**. A matched correctness screen must preserve cold-TT resets, deadlines and error handling, and compare every CP/mate, native WDL, calibrated value, move and node count against this baseline before timing it in another quiet CPU window. Any sparse SF policy correction remains a separate selector and training experiment.
