# Ceres active128 frozen-source quality gate

**Readout, 2026-09-30.** The fixed32 and active128 source screens used the same 1,024-game roster, model SHA-256 `44aa02c775456f18ed464e33fc37b8e4abf58d7bf8f4cfb3ff19492e32e56df3`, and source plan. Their session descriptions differ only in `backend.batch_size` (32 versus 128). Both closed source banks passed separate all-output CPU audits. The active128 parent driver failed on a `/proc/io` race after source completion; its separately recovered CPU terminal and source child passed. These are **source-only, unqualified** archives, with no corpus, target, training, or 500M credit.

The fixed32 child published 127,314 accepted rows in 4,183 physical calls (6,542 padding rows; child wall time 827.182 s). The active128 child published 129,546 accepted rows in 1,154 calls (18,166 padding rows; child wall time 652.745 s). The actor followed its own model outputs, so the two archives do not contain identical trajectories and their wall times are not a same-input throughput comparison. The active128 disjoint actor phase receipt attributes 241.174 s to root preparation and outcome, 279.099 s to sink plus readback, 105.652 s to the bound session call, 10.326 s to CPU encode/gather, and 12.740 s to move choice/application. Root and sink CPU work therefore matter alongside model calls.

We compared the **52,820 distinct exact feed inputs** shared by the two banks. Every shared feed had the same game/ply coordinate, FEN, and sorted compact/Leela legal support. Each bank's repeated feed inputs had identical raw heads within that bank. The saved raw legal logits and both value heads differ across the batch profiles; the policy-logit per-feed maximum absolute difference has mean 0.014054, p99 0.051758 and maximum 0.8515625. This raw-logit statistic alone is not the numerical gate.

For the actual selected-label transform, we applied softmax at **T=0.5** to legal logits in ascending compact order and stored FP16 policy probabilities. The Ceres WDL used FP16 of **0.6 softmax(value/T=0.55) + 0.4 softmax(value2/T=1.5)**. Across shared feeds, compact top-1 agreement is **52,643/52,820 = 99.6649%** (177 disagreements), above the frozen 99% minimum. Policy total variation has mean **0.002426675**, p99 **0.013818943** (below 0.02) and maximum **0.226678640**, exceeding the frozen **0.05 maximum**. Exactly five rows exceed 0.05. Calibrated WDL total variation has mean **0.000395162**, p99 **0.004333496** (below 0.01) and maximum **0.039916992** (below 0.05). **The existing frozen numerical-profile budget fails solely on maximum policy TV; active128 is held from numerical-profile promotion.** The banked comparison checks shared inputs rather than every divergent trajectory and says nothing about playing strength.

An independently coded saved-Zarr spotcheck reconstructed the complete 175-plane FP16 input and 64×137 Ceres feed for all five policy-TV breaches. Full input and feed bytes, feed hashes, FEN/history, and legal maps matched between profiles in every case; the recomputed FP16 TVs matched the full comparison records. The five feeds occurred once per bank, all in refill games, at game-local plies 3, 18, 38, 41 and 95; piece counts were 31, 29, 23, 22 and 10. The largest case has policy TV 0.226679 at game 10533, ply 18: its compact top-1 stays the same while a secondary legal logit changes substantially. Matching input bytes and a session profile differing only in batch size establish an observed batch-shape-associated numerical difference. They do not identify a defective GPU kernel or establish fixed32 as ground truth.

A separate, bounded CPU profile replayed one hash-pinned active128 shard with 8,099 rows. Root preparation took 9.461 s within a 10.819 s replay; nested strict-outcome time was 1.596 s, including 1.437 s in the history-sensitive threefold check. Stored-feed conversion took 2.908 s, position fingerprints 1.578 s, and full-history board snapshots 0.635 s; these timers overlap and include wrapper overhead. A full independent sink `verify_shard` of that shard took 3.918 s without profiling, while a whole-ZIP SHA-256 took 0.035 s. The current sink fully verifies before atomic rename and the ledger fully verifies the renamed ZIP again; the final shard has a third readback. An **isolated CPU-only candidate** retains the pre-rename independent verification, binds its exact receipt to inode/size, verifies the post-rename SHA-256, and keeps full verification for external receipts. It passed independent source review, focused tests and closed-shard ledger parity, but it is not part of either frozen source run and has no measured integrated throughput credit.

The [compact evidence record](evidence/2026-09-30-ceres-active128-frozen-quality.json) pins both source/audit identities, all 52,820 comparison rows, the five full-byte checks, budget and source/reader timings. A future active128 numerical retry needs a new preregistered gate or a change that passes the existing maximum-TV rule; this readout does not waive that rule.
